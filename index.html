Days 7–16

Days 7-16 (31 Oct to about 9 Nov): ensembles agree strongly on a blocked pattern early in the period. ECMWF HRES has the high over the North Sea at about 1027-1032 hPa on Saturday 31st, and west to north-west of Scotland at 1030-1034 hPa by 3 Nov. The ECMWF ENS mean centres a 1030 hPa high over the North Sea and Scandinavia at day 10. The AIFS ENS mean has 1030 hPa over Scandinavia and the Baltic, with only 4-8 hPa spread over the UK. JMA (1035-1040 hPa over Germany and Poland), AIFS and GEM are similar. The GEFS mean is less blocked, keeping the Atlantic trough closer to Ireland and the UK on the edge of the high. For Edinburgh that means mostly dry weather (ensemble means 0.1-0.5 mm per 6 h), light winds and temperatures near normal: daytime about 9-11 °C, nights 3-6 °C, colder in clear spells. Ensemble mean 850 hPa temperatures sit around +1 to +3 °C, close to climatology, but the cold tail widens. ECMWF p10 falls to about -3 °C by 6-8 Nov, GEFS p10 to about -6 °C by 9 Nov and GEM ENS p10 to about -4 °C, a sign that some members drift a cooler continental or north-easterly flow towards Scotland. Ground frost and fog become possible. ECMWF ENS gives 2-4% of members an air frost on 4-5 Nov, with the lowest member at -1.4 °C. By day 15 the AIFS ENS spread rises to 12-18 hPa over the UK and North Atlantic, and the AIFS deterministic run brings a deep Atlantic trough west of Ireland with the UK in a southerly flow. Whether and when the block gives way is therefore low confidence beyond about 5 Nov.

Weather regimes

NAO+ has 100% probability on 24-26 Oct, matching the current storm, and 70-75% on 27-28 Oct. Blocking then dominates at 70-94% from 29 Oct to about 4 Nov, still 50-60% to about 8 Nov. Through mid-November the mix is roughly 30-35% blocking, about 20% NAO+, 15-25% Atlantic Ridge and 5-15% NAO-. By late November and early December NAO+ climbs to 30-40%, with blocking about 25-30% and NAO- 10-15%. For Edinburgh this means a dry, settled early November (frost and fog rather than snow, since the block's cold air sits over the continent), then increasingly westerly, mild and wetter weather towards December. NAO- and Atlantic Ridge, the regimes that bring northerly or easterly cold to Scotland, never exceed about 15-25%.

Polar vortex

The 10 hPa zonal-mean wind falls sharply from about 22 m/s now to an ensemble mean near 11 m/s around 6 Nov, against a climatology of about 25 m/s. That is a notably weak early-season vortex, consistent with the tropospheric blocking. Only a handful of members (under about 5%) briefly reverse to slightly easterly winds (around 5-6 Nov, 13 Nov, 20 Nov and 27 Nov), so there is no meaningful sudden-warming signal. The mean then recovers strongly to about 37 m/s by 7 Dec, above the roughly 31 m/s climatology, which favours a westerly, NAO-positive early winter rather than a stratosphere-driven cold spell.

A very strong El Niño and a vortex expected to recover to above-normal strength by early December make a cold winter overall unlikely. A colder late-winter spell with some snow remains plausible.

Drivers
  • Very strong El Niño: the SEAS5 Niño 3.4 plume rises from about +3.6 °C in October to about +4.1-4.3 °C in December, and is still +2.4 to +3.8 °C in April. This tends to favour an active Atlantic jet early, with an NAO-negative tendency in late winter.
  • SEAS5 NAO medians by month: October about +2, November about -0.4, December about 0, January about -1.6, February about -2.5, March about -0.8, all with very wide spread.
  • SEAS5 DJF MSLP: +2 to +4 hPa over Greenland and Iceland, -2 to -4 hPa over the central Atlantic, -1 to -2 hPa over the UK. This is a cyclonic, wet setup for Scotland rather than a cold easterly one.
  • C3S 500 hPa heights: strong positive anomaly over Labrador and SW Greenland (+4 to +8 dam in DJF and JFM), near zero over the UK, positive over the Mediterranean.
  • Stratospheric vortex: weak in early November (mean about 11 m/s against about 25 m/s climatology) with no robust reversal, then recovering to above normal (about 37 m/s) by early December. This supports the November block but works against an early-winter cold regime.
  • Warm background climate and warm surrounding seas: SEAS5, C3S and GloSea all show the UK in the upper tercile or +0.5 to +1 °C across the season.
  • Cool SST and temperature anomaly south of Greenland in the C3S and SEAS5 maps, consistent with an active Atlantic storm track.
