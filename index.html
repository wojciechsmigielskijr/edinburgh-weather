Polar vortex: will the winds reverse?

How fast the stratospheric polar vortex is spinning: the average west-to-east wind right round the 60°N circle, about 30 km up (10 hPa). Below zero the wind has turned easterly, which is a sudden stratospheric warming (SSW). Most SSWs are followed 1–3 weeks later by a better chance of blocked, cold weather here. A strong vortex usually means mild westerlies.

Chance of a sudden stratospheric warming

high

Strength now: the 60°N 10 hPa wind is 12.3 m/s (ECMWF analysis; GFS 11.5-12.2 m/s). Against NASA's 1979-2026 climatology for 31 October it is far below the 10th percentile (17.6 m/s) and only 1 m/s above the record minimum (11.3 m/s); the mean is 24.2 m/s. Severe-weather.eu calls it weaker than 9 years in 10, and weatheriscool's ERA5 weakest for the date is 11.6 m/s (2016). At 50 hPa the wind is 7.7-8.0 m/s, at the NASA record minimum for the date (7.5 m/s).

Early-warning signs fired strongly:

  • The 100 hPa heat flux at 60°N peaked at 46-48 K m/s on 29 October, with a second pulse of 38-41 K m/s on 3 November.
  • On severe-weather.eu (45-75°N) it is 24.2 K m/s now against a mean of 10.5 and a 90th percentile of 16.8, peaking at 28.6 on 2 November.
  • NASA's 45-day mean is above its 90th percentile.
  • The flux then collapses to 4-17 K m/s by 9-12 November.
  • The 10 hPa polar-cap temperature rises only 4-6 °C, to -55 to -57 °C on 8 November, but that is above the 1958-2025 maximum for those dates.

Reversal:

  • The ECMWF ENS mean turns easterly on 5 November and bottoms at -4.0 m/s on 10 November; 39% of members are still easterly on 14 November.
  • GEOS-FP goes easterly on 5 November, to -5.0 m/s on 8 November.

Shape: on 1-2 November wave 2 peaks at 578-607 m in every model. In ECMWF its ridges tilt west with height: the 500 hPa ridge is at 35-41°E (Urals), the 10 hPa ridge at about 5°E, and the partner ridge 180° away near Alaska, a tilt of -30° to -36°. That is the structure of a split.

  • The hebweather GFS, ECMWF and GEFS comparison for 4 November and the NOAA GFS map at +120 h both show two lows, over Canada and over the Siberian Arctic: split-like.
  • After that, wave 2 collapses (60-100 m by 10-12 November) and wave 1 grows to 758-794 m by 12-15 November. The ECMWF centroid is at 60-63°N to 10 November. That is a displacement towards the Atlantic side.
  • The ECMWF 10 hPa maps put the low over Hudson Bay on 5 November and over Greenland and Baffin on 10 November. The ECMWF ENS mean, GFS and NOAA +384 h maps centre it over Scandinavia and the Barents Sea by 15-16 November.
  • The machine-learning AIFS 10 hPa maps (Europe sector only) put the cold core over Greenland, Iceland and Scandinavia on 5-10 November and over the Barents Sea by 15 November. That is consistent with a displaced, not split, vortex.

Depth: the two measures disagree.

  • 100 hPa polar-cap height: +1.76 sd now. Deterministic peaks are ECMWF HRES +2.44, GFS +2.31, UKMO +2.24, GEM +2.12 and AIFS +2.07 sd. 100% of members in ECMWF ENS, AIFS ENS, GEFS and GEPS reach +1 sd. On this measure the event is deep.
  • 50 hPa wind: stays westerly. Ever easterly there are 2% of ECMWF ENS, 0% of GEFS, 0% of AIFS ENS and 3% of AIGEFS, with deterministic minima of +2.3 to +5.2 m/s. The GFS section takes easterlies only down to 20-30 hPa on 5 November.

The deep cap lasts longer in some ensembles than others. Share of members still above +1 sd at 100 hPa:

  • ECMWF ENS: 51% on 13 November, 45% on 14 November.
  • AIFS ENS: 84% on 14 November.
  • GEPS: 57% on 16 November.
  • GEFS: 16% from 13 November.

Further down, coupling is modest. The 500 hPa cap peaks at +0.94 sd in ECMWF HRES and +1.29 in GFS on 7 November; the GFS section's 1000 hPa cap reaches +0.86 on 7 November, then all fall to near zero by 9-10 November.

A major reversal in the first week of November is almost unprecedented. It looks short at 10 hPa, roughly 5-14 November, but it is deep on the 100 hPa polar cap. The troposphere is already going that way: GEFS forecasts the AO at -1.4 and the NAO at about -1 by 10-13 November. Much of that comes from the same Ural-Scandinavian block that drove the wave pulse, not from a downward response. About two thirds of events like this couple down, typically 1-3 weeks later. That puts Edinburgh's window for extra blocking and colder interludes at about mid-November to early December, and ECMWF's blocking regime rising to about 40% on 3-7 December fits that. Several things temper it: - the 50 hPa wind stays westerly; - the QBO is westerly (+12.9 m/s at 30 hPa); - the wave driving collapses after 8 November; - ECMWF rebuilds the vortex to above normal (about 42 m/s) by mid-December, which would favour mild, wet westerlies later in December; - the cold source upwind is thin: at most 8% of ECMWF members have -8 °C at 850 hPa over Stockholm or Oslo by 14 November, with almost no snow cover; - the North Sea is +1.1 °C above its 1991-2020 normal (58% of cells above +1 °C, OISST). For the city, a blocked spell in the next month most likely means frost, fog and raw, grey easterlies, or a chilly north-westerly with snow showers on the Pentlands, rather than lowland snow.
