The city gets showers of rain, hail and sleet, with frost on clear nights. The Pentlands get snow showers and a thin cover on the tops. Five nights at -3 °C in a row stay unlikely in a showery wind.
  • 22 November to 12 December (medium): a blocking high over Scandinavia, first with a low west of Ireland. The wind is south-easterly from a mild continent, so the city stays near normal, with fog, frost and cold rain on fronts. The Pentlands and Moorfoots get battleground snow if fronts stall against the block. A true easterly with snow showers needs the low south of Scotland and -8 °C air over Denmark, which only 12% of members show.
  • February (low): an easterly on the north side of a low over the UK and western Europe. Seven of eight seasonal centres show a strong Arctic high, and the SEAS5 NAO median is near -2.9. But SEAS5 alone keeps 65% of members warmer than its own February climate. The warm North Sea needs air aloft near -8 °C for snow at the coast. The city gets snow that lies a day or two at best, and the Pentlands get lying snow for days.
  • 17-31 December (low): a ridge near the UK with a trough over Scandinavia, or a second vortex disruption. The EC46 500 hPa map for 21 to 28 December has this shape, but NAO+ is most likely on 13 to 21 December. One or two EC46 members reach 0 m/s at 10 hPa near late December. The city gets frost under the ridge and cold showers if the wind turns north, and the hills get snow showers.
  • March (low): late frost and short snowfalls in northerly or easterly outbreaks. The SEAS5 NAO median is near -2.4, and the point data give 8 frost days against 5.7. The city gets frost and brief snow, and the hills get lying snow after each outbreak.
  • January (low): mostly westerly or north-westerly flow behind a vortex that rebuilds to about 38 m/s. Four centres put a low near the UK, wet and near normal, and NCEP and Meteo-France put it over Scandinavia. The city gets rain, sleet in showers and frost on clear nights. Snow to low levels needs a block, and the warming chance is 18%.

A check on the odds

Each chance has an exact definition, so a check is possible. A cold winter: December to February as a whole is colder than its 1991-2020 normal at the location (ERA5-Land daily mean temperature). A cold spell: at least one run of 5 or more days in a row from 1 November to 31 March. Each day of the run has a minimum of -3 °C or lower, or at least 5 cm of lying snow at the location (ERA5-Land). ERA5-Land is a reanalysis on a 9 km grid, so "lying snow" is for the grid square, not for your street. The table shows how often each occurred.

A wet winter: December to February as a whole has more precipitation than its 1991-2020 mean at the location (ERA5, 0.25° grid). Sudden warmings, month by month

A dated call beyond the models' range is easy to praise after the event. For this reason, these calls get scores, as the winter odds do. The event: a major sudden stratospheric warming starts in that month. On a day in the month, the zonal-mean wind at 60°N, 10 hPa (NASA MERRA-2, and GEOS-FP for this season) turns easterly. This occurs at least 20 westerly days after any earlier event. Before 30 April, the wind is westerly again for 10 days in a row (Charlton and Polvani). A reversal with no recovery is the final warming, and it does not count. A call counts for a month only if it came before the start of the month. Drivers

  • Polar vortex: a major sudden stratospheric warming started on 10 November. NASA GEOS shows -2.9 m/s at 60°N and 10 hPa on 11 November, a record low for the date. The 10 hPa wind is near 5 m/s on 16 November, below the weakest value for the date in the ERA5 record. This is the earliest major warming in the record, on NASA MERRA-2 since 1979/80.
  • Depth: the two measures disagree. The 50 hPa wind stays westerly at 7 to 12 m/s, near the 10th percentile. The 100 hPa cap reached +2.19 sd on 4 November. It returns above +1 sd in 61% of ECMWF ENS, 88% of AIFS ENS and 52% of GEPS members, but 0% of GEFS. So the event is deep.
  • Surface answer: ECMWF ENS takes the 500 hPa cap to +1 sd in half of its members from 25 November, and 59% on 26 November. But the low sits west of Ireland and the high over Scandinavia, so Edinburgh gets a south-easterly near normal, not deep cold.
  • Shape: the ECMWF ENS mean puts the vortex off the pole towards Scandinavia on 19 November. It stretches the vortex from Canada to Siberia on 24 November. The wave-2 ridges tilt west with height, but the 10 hPa wind rises, so this is a stretch, not a split.
  • Wave forcing: the 100 hPa heat flux peaked at 46.4 K m/s on 29 October, mostly in wave 2. A second pulse of 29 to 38 K m/s comes on 21 to 23 November. It meets a vortex that rebuilds, so it reverses the vortex in no member.
  • Pacific chain: the mountain torque over East Asia reached +4.0 sd on 8 November, and angular momentum rose from +0.3 sd to +2.8 sd by 15 November. The PNA is +1.6, and ECMWF ENS puts 61% of members at an EPO of -1 or below on 21 November. The chain follows in part.
  • El Nino: very strong and east Pacific, with the ONI at +2.46, the RONI at +1.97 and a record MEI v2 of +2.53. The nearest same weeks are 1997 by flavour and 2015 by Nino 3.4. The class leans mild here and in Central England, mostly within chance.
  • QBO: westerly, +15.9 m/s at 30 hPa and +13.5 m/s at 50 hPa in October. It favours a fast rebuild and fewer mid-winter disruptions, so it lowers the cold odds.
  • MJO: weak, phase 4 at amplitude 1.31 on 11 November. ECMWF ENS and GEFS keep it near phases 3 and 4 and mostly inside the circle, so neither pushes a negative NAO.
  • Angular momentum: +2.8 sd on 15 November, with the Global Wind Oscillation in phase 6 at 2.95, the El Nino state with an extended Pacific jet.
  • Sea temperatures: the North Sea is +0.77 °C, so coast snow stays marginal. The tripole is -0.61, a weak positive-NAO sign. The Barents and Kara Seas are +1.73 °C, linked to a weaker vortex.
  • Snow and cold upwind: Eurasian snow is 14.55 million km2, rank 41 of 61, below the 16.06 normal. Oslo has snow, Stockholm and Helsinki do not, and Copenhagen reaches -8 °C at 850 hPa in at most 12% of members.
  • Extended range: the ECMWF regimes give Scandinavian blocking on 22 to 27 November and 7 to 12 December, and NAO+ on 13 to 21 December. EC46 keeps Arctic pressure near +0.4 sd in late November, then near 0.
  • Seasonal models: the November issue predates the warming. It puts the UK at 0 to +1 °C, but seven of eight centres show a strongly negative NAO in February. I weight the NAO signal up and the mild maps down.
  • Analogs: outside the record, with no measurable skill, so they get no weight. The nearest El Nino and QBO winters average +0.2 °C, and the 4 winters with an early warming -0.3 °C, both within chance.

East Pacific Oscillation (EPO)

The EPO compares the 500 hPa height near Alaska (55–65°N) with the height farther south (20–35°N), both at 160–125°W. A positive EPO means a trough near Alaska and mild Pacific air. A negative EPO means a ridge over Alaska. This often sends cold air into North America and can make the upward wave flux into the vortex stronger. This page calculates the index itself, so it can differ a little from the EPO on other sites. The days match the heat flux charts above, so you can compare when the two change.
